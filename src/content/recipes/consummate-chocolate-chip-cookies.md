Large chocolate chip cookies with bittersweet chocolate disks and a sprinkle of
sea salt.

## Ingredients

- 280 g unsalted butter, at room temperature (1¼ cups / 2½ sticks)
- 240 g light brown sugar (1¼ cups)
- 225 g granulated sugar (1 cup plus 2 tbsp)
- 2 large eggs
- 10 ml vanilla extract (2 tsp)
- 1¼ tsp baking soda
- 1½ tsp baking powder
- 1½ tsp coarse or kosher salt
- 445 g all-purpose flour (3½ cups plus 2 tsp)
- 565 g bittersweet chocolate disks or fèves, at least 60% (1¼ lb)
- Sea salt, for sprinkling

## Method

1. **Cream the butter and sugars** with a hand or stand mixer until very light
   and fluffy, 3–4 minutes. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla,
   and scrape down the bowl.
2. **Add the leaveners and salt.** Sprinkle the baking soda, baking powder, and
   salt over the dough and mix until combined.
3. **Add the flour all at once** and mix in short bursts, only until it nearly
   disappears. Don't overmix.
4. **Fold in the chocolate**, trying not to break the disks.
5. **Rest the dough.** Cover the bowl and refrigerate at least 24 hours and up
   to 72 hours.
6. **Heat the oven** to 175°C (350°F) and line large baking sheets with
   parchment or nonstick mats.
7. **Portion the dough** into 100 g (3½ oz) balls. They will look oversized.
   Tuck any chocolate on the surface back into the dough, space the balls well
   apart, and sprinkle each with a few flakes of sea salt.
8. **Bake 12–17 minutes**, until golden all over. Check the first batch early,
   since spread varies.
9. **Cool** 10 minutes on the trays, then move to racks.

## Notes

The cookies spread to about 13 cm (5 inches), so only a few fit on each sheet.
Bake in batches and keep the remaining dough in the fridge between them.

The 24–72 hour rest makes this a make-ahead dough. Start it at least a day
before you want to bake.

Coarse kosher salts vary in density by brand. Morton is roughly twice as salty
by volume as Diamond Crystal, so use a little less with Morton.

Disks or fèves melt into wide layers through the cookie. Chopped bar chocolate
works in their place; standard chips hold their shape and give a different
texture.
