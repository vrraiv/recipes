A dense, buttery purée of waxy new potatoes.

## Ingredients

- 1 kg new potatoes, peeled
- 150 g unsalted butter, cubed and softened
- 180 ml whole milk or 35% cream
- 60 g cream cheese or full-fat sour cream
- 2 garlic cloves, peeled and halved (optional)
- 1 tbsp salt, for the cooking water
- ½ tsp fine salt, to finish

## Method

1. **Peel and cut.** The skins slip off easily. Cut into even 4 cm chunks so
   they cook at the same rate.
2. **Start in cold water.** Cover by 3 cm, add the cooking salt, bring to a
   bare simmer rather than a rolling boil.
3. **Simmer 18 minutes**, until a knife meets zero resistance. Undercooking is
   the main cause of lumps — err soft.
4. **Warm the dairy.** Meanwhile heat the milk with the garlic over low heat
   for 10 minutes without boiling. Remove the garlic before using.
5. **Drain and dry.** Return the potatoes to the empty hot pot over low heat
   for 60–90 seconds, shaking occasionally. Less surface water means room for
   more butter.
6. **Rice while hot.** Use a ricer or food mill. A hand masher works if you
   press gently and stop as soon as the lumps go. Never a blender or food
   processor — waxy potatoes turn to glue in seconds.
7. **Fold in the butter** off the heat, a few cubes at a time, until each
   addition disappears. Fold, don't whip.
8. **Loosen with the warm dairy.** Add the cream cheese, then the milk in three
   additions, until looser than you'd normally serve — it firms as it cools.
9. **Season in two portions.** Set aside the unsalted portion first, then stir
   the finishing salt into the rest.

## Notes

Serve warm rather than hot, and skip pepper or herbs if anyone at the table is
recovering from dental work — specks lodge in healing sockets and hot food can
disturb the clots.

Leftovers stiffen in the fridge. Reheat gently with a splash of milk over low
heat, stirring as little as possible.

No ricer and no food mill? Press the cooked potatoes through a fine sieve with
a spatula. Still better than a masher.
